# Hosted qualification

CI cannot check one thing: that the library works against hosted Reactor. These checks do, for money, so they run only when a maintainer authorizes the spend. They gated 0.3.0: the published 0.3.0-rc.0 failed them on three library causes, and 0.3.0 released the fixes after they passed from a checkout ([the record](./evidence/0.3.0-rc.0/summary.md)).

`main.ts` is the command line. Each check is one Effect program over the public API (`Checks.ts`); a rehearsal runs the same program against `ReactorTest` instead of hosted Reactor, for free.

### Tour

`tour` walks the public API's provider calls through one session, so that each is seen on hosted Reactor once without a session of its own. Its session is capped at 90 s, reserves $3.15 at H3's per-second rate, and its work ends within 80 s of the allocation; the plan ends about 40 s in.

- **Each phase on its own.** A phase judges its own criteria under its own time limit. One that fails is a failed criterion named after it, with its failure's tag and message, and the phases after it still run where they can.
- **Tokens.** The session is created on `CoordinatorClient.tokens`, not a fixed grant, with tokens that live 30 s. Every token is proven and accepted before it can allocate: the creating one by its cap, each one bound to the session by Reactor's echo of the bind. At the refresh point (a quarter of the token's life before it expires), whatever the phases before it have reached, the session uploads a picture on its own token, and must mint a token bound to itself before the creating one expires. Two seconds after that expiry it reconnects on the refreshed token. A token that fails its proof is recorded as refused and never used.
- **Judged, as H3's schema or the SDK documents them.** The canvas asked for and its size in `canvas_accepted` and the state; clip 1's correlated acceptance, lifecycle, live video, audio, `has_reference_audio` and metadata echo; clip 2 accepted on clip 1's uploads with nothing uploaded again, reporting one image and one audio reference; the queue read after a `move` to the front; a stop acknowledged, the playing clip stopped and nothing started with autoplay off until a `play` of the clip it names; a prompt past the text budget failing its clip with `clip_failed`, which the clip's operation reports as `ClipEnded`; a reset stopping the playing clip and leaving both queues empty; popped clips never built or started; the reconnect making the next generation, H3 answering on it with the ready clip kept, and fresh frames on it; the API key's termination and the session's own close confirmed; and, only once the end is confirmed, an attach to the ended session failing, and the key finding no unknown session: its read failing and its termination confirmed absent.
- **Recorded, not judged.** The seed H3 echoes and its default before and after; a position-zero clip's place in the queue; each queue command's round trip and `refresh`'s outcome; whether the moved clip headed the generation queue when popped, which makes it the build in flight only by inference, and when clip 2 was built after that pop; what `reset` restores; the DELETE statuses, and which refusal the attach and the unknown session's read met, with their HTTP statuses; whether the deployment records (Reactor leaves recording to each deployment: `RecorderDisabled`, or the clip's markers and its download's size, never its content); and what the SDK reads from the echo of a three-session token and an uncapped one, which mint nothing that is used.
- **Rehearsed.** ReactorTest answers as a disabled recorder does; a rehearsal test turns its recorder on so the download runs too, and another refuses clip 1's image, after which the later phases still run and the session still ends.

### Adoption

`adoption` takes one session over the ways `takeover`, `resume` and `tokens` do in three, and reaches what none of them does: a raw attach that closes without ending the session, `H3Source.resume` on hosted H3, and the isolated native host. It reserves $2.625 at H3's per-second rate, so its `--budget-usd` is at least that and at most its $4.20 ceiling.

- **The owner.** A paid run starts it under Node (`node` on the `PATH`, 22.18 or newer) with each connection's native peer in a child process of its own (`NativePeer.layerIsolated`, which needs a Node parent); `node.ts` lets Node run the harness from its TypeScript sources. The owner reports its runtime and peer, and the evidence keeps them. It reports its session as soon as it allocates, before it connects, so a session whose owner fails before streaming is still ended with the key; its kill is `SIGKILL`, and the check waits at most 5 s for it to exit. A rehearsal runs the owner in its own process on ReactorTest's peers, so the isolated host runs only in a paid run; `preflight` checks it for free beforehand.
- **The timeline.** From the owner's allocation: its first 15 s clip starts about 10 s in, and the owner is killed at 12 s. Its second clip lasts 15 s too, so its air runs to about 41 s: paid `tokens` found a 5 s queued clip already playing 24 s into its run. The raw attach is ready within 5 s, reads 2 s of frames and closes. The session is read every half second while nothing is connected, for at least 1 s and until the owner's 20 s token has expired, which was minted before the owner started. The resume then adopts the session, about 20 s in, reads its frames while one of the owner's clips still plays, and past its first token's refresh point (a quarter of the token's 12 s before it expires) enqueues a clip with a reference image and reference audio. The refusals and the close follow about 32 s in, well inside the 65 s work deadline. The kill waits for the owner to stream, so a slower hosted build moves every later step with it.
- **Judged.** The raw attach is ready within 5 s of the kill, names the owner's playing clip, reads the queued clip's metadata, sends no enqueue and receives fresh frames; its close attempts no termination, and the session still reads live after it. The resume starts after the creating token expired, owns the session, names either of the owner's clips playing (the first may have ended by then), receives fresh frames, and a refreshed token carries its clip's upload, which reports `has_reference_audio`. A token past its expiry is refused with 401 and one without the bind with 403, as authentication documents. Closing the resumed source ends the session, confirmed and `CLOSED`.
- **Recorded.** What the coordinator says of the session while nothing is connected, with the time since the last connection closed: Reactor documents only that the session waits 30 s for a connection, not what it reads meanwhile.
- **Each step on its own.** Every step runs under its own time limit. One that fails is a failed criterion named after it, with its failure's tag and message, and the steps after it run where they still make sense: the refusals are still read and the resumed source is still closed.

### Show

`show` runs the rest of `Playout`'s provider-facing behaviour in one playout: 75 s sessions, a 30 s renewal lead, 5 to 8 s of filler runway, a cut lane above the lineup's lane, a `replace` lane and a `skip` lane below it, and `maxModerations: 2`, so the planned loss by moderation does not end the show. Each phase judges its own criteria, and a failure, or running past its session's work deadline, is a failed criterion named after it while the later phases still run.

- Session 1: its connection is cut, and it must read ready again and show a fresh frame within 20 s, with no replacement opened. The playout must report the reconnect once, with the session's ready between its `Reconnecting` and `Reconnected`, and an `afterMillis` within 500 ms of the time between them. Then the `edits` sequence and its criteria; then one planned switch to session 2.
- Session 2: an `Asap` item airs ahead of one waiting, cues fire within 500 ms of their offsets, and an item due `At` an instant airs, never before it. It is then ended under a playing clip, by moderation with `--moderation-prompt-file` and with the API key without: the clip on air fails as lost, a moderated item fails as moderated, and a Ready one is carried to the next session.
- Session 3: the carried clip airs; a cut lane's clip stops a lower lane's playing clip with one `stop`, starts next and is not stopped itself; a `replace` lane's new item takes its waiting item's place; a `skip` lane refuses an item while one waits; the drain completes.
- Throughout: each item airs on the session on air at its start, every session gives video, and filler keeps the air covered. The playout never starves outside the cut connection and the loss. No clip's end, filler's included, goes 1.5 s without the next start (hosted clips followed each other within 40 ms, and a cut's within 150 ms), outside the cut connection and from the loss until the lanes begin, while session 3 builds c1 and then the long clip in H3's one build slot. The gap at the planned switch is recorded, not judged: `Playout` promises only that the switch follows the grace. The playout's state names each clip as its start is reported, with that start, and the show's own reader of the picture and sound never falls behind.

The evidence records each filler clip's start and end, every gap between clips, each reconnect the playout reported with the length its source measured, any reader that fell behind, each failed item's reason, and how the state read at each start.

### Showreel

`showreel` records footage of hosted H3 for the README and the documentation. Its one session is capped at 70 s and reserves $2.45 at H3's per-second rate, so its `--budget-usd` is at least that and at most its $4.20 ceiling. A paid run needs ffmpeg on the `PATH` with the libx264, aac, png and gif encoders and the fps, scale, split, palettegen and paletteuse filters, and refuses before it claims its evidence file, and so before it spends anything, without them.

- **On air.** A playout over `H3Source` airs five scenes of 8 s back to back in one lane: a red vintage convertible on a salt flat at dawn, close on its chrome, through a canyon, along a coast road and, from the air, at the horizon. Every prompt ends with the same description of the look, so five clips built on their own read as one reel. The session holds the last frame at each seam. Planned from paid timing, the first scene airs about 7 s after allocation, each later one is built while the one before it airs, and the last ends about 47 s in; the session closes about 48 s in, 12 s before the work deadline and 22 s before the cap.
- **The recording.** From the first scene's start to the last one's end, the playout's decoded picture and sound go to ffmpeg as they arrive, as H.264 (CRF 18) and AAC. The file keeps the clock's timing: 24 frames a second, each the latest frame to have arrived by its time, so a held frame at a seam lasts as long in the file as it did on air; the sound starts with the picture, and a block the host dropped becomes silence. A frame of another size or format than the first is dropped and counted, since raw video keeps one; hosted H3 has changed a session's size once its canvas was set. Up to 128 MiB of picture waits for ffmpeg, about 1.3 s of hosted H3's 1344x768 frames, before the reader waits on it.
- **After the session.** Once the session has closed, ffmpeg makes `poster.png` from the middle of the third scene, and `loop.gif` from the 8 s centred on the seam into it: 720 pixels wide at 12 frames a second, with a palette made from its own frames.
- **Judged.** Every scene built, started and ended, in order; every seam measured (its gap on air, its longest pause and its dark frames); no scene's end going 1.5 s without the next start, as `show` judges it; no reader of the picture or sound falling behind; the reel recorded: ffmpeg exited 0 and its frames cover the scenes' time on air, with sound when the session offered it; the poster and the loop made; and the session confirmed ended.
- **The files.** They go beside the run's evidence, in a directory named as the evidence file is: `<ledger>/<stamp>-showreel-paid-<run>/` holds `reel.mp4`, `poster.png` and `loop.gif`. Git ignores these directories under `evidence/` ([`.gitignore`](./.gitignore)); the evidence keeps only the files' names and sizes and where the poster and the loop were taken from.

ReactorTest sends no sound between clips, so a rehearsal's sound ends 0.3 s before its picture; hosted H3 sent sound continuously in paid runs, so nothing is added for it. `rehearse showreel --ledger <dir>` keeps the files. A rehearsal whose first scene's build fails judges that the scene never started and closes its session at once, 8 s after allocation.

### Unconnected

`unconnected` asks hosted Reactor what the library cannot know: what becomes of a session nothing connects to. Reactor's Sessions page says billing runs "from creation", and its Billing page that the meter "starts the moment your session reaches `ready`"; neither says whether Reactor ends such a session, or when. The answer bounds what an allocation costs when its creator crashes before connecting, or never learns that its create succeeded. The check also asks what a second create on a spent single-session token answers, which decides whether a create whose outcome is unknown may be sent again. It watches its session past the cap, so it reserves that session for 155 s, the spent token's for 59 s and one more, which that token's second create might allocate, for 56 s: $9.45 at H3's per-second rate, and $10.50, its ceiling, in whole minutes, since neither of the short ones may run into a second minute. The $10 total admits it only at the per-second rate.

- **Two tokens.** Each is for one session capped at 60 s, and both are minted before anything is allocated. Every create goes through the session's own signaling seam (`coordinator.signaling(...).create`), which only allocates: nothing ever connects to any of the sessions.
- **The watched session.** The first token allocates it, and nothing else is sent on that token until the key has ended the session, so nothing the check sends can end it before the key does.
- **The spent token.** As soon as the watched session is allocated, the second token allocates a session of its own, which spends it, and as soon as that create is answered, a second create goes out on the same token, as a retry of a create whose outcome is unknown would. Its reason, the SDK's outcome, its HTTP status, its body's key names and any codes in it are recorded, never its text. Codes are looked for under any key, two levels down and in a list's first three entries. A code is letters, digits, `_` and `-` only, at most 40 of them, so a UUID but no long digest or token. It holds no IPv4 address however its numbers are joined, and no doubled `-` or `_` or six groups of hex digits joined, so no IPv6 or MAC address either. Other text is kept by its length only. Nothing is kept under a key that is itself no code, or whose words name an address or a secret, such as `host`, `endpoints`, `port`, `IPAddress`, `api_key` or `password`: however the key is cased or joined, in the singular or the plural. So no URL, IP address or dotted host name reaches the evidence, which is committed, nor anything a key calls an address or a secret; and as with every save, one that would hold the API key or a token writes nothing and stops the run. A bare name such as `gpu-7`, or a bare number, still reads as a code under any other key: only a key could tell it for a host. A reply naming the spent token's session again allocated nothing, so that session is the only one held. When the spent token's first create fails, no second create goes out: a token that may be unspent would not ask the question.
- **The holds.** Once the second create is answered, the key reads the spent token's session, and any the second create allocated, every 0.5 s until a read finds its capabilities and a transport, for 5 s at most. It holds each 10 s past that read, or 10 s past the wait if none found it ready, and then ends each, side by side so none waits on another's reads or tries, and confirms each end. So each short session runs a known time past its ready, and a known time past its allocation, and the dashboard's duration for it says which Reactor bills from. A session a read finds `CLOSED` is not held: the key ends it at once. No hold runs past the session's own in the plan: the key begins to end a session 25 s before that runs out (22 s for its three tries, and 3 s for a last read in the wait and for timers), whatever its wait left, so each short session ends within the minute it started.
- **Side by side.** The watch and the spent token's creates and holds run side by side (`Effect.all` with unbounded concurrency), so no read of the watched session waits on a create, on a hold or on the key's ends of what it made: with a second create hanging to its 15 s deadline, the rehearsal's reads still find the session `ACTIVE` within 2.5 s of allocation. A failure neither records, such as a save that fails, stops both, and the key then ends whatever the check holds.
- **The reads.** The API key reads the watched session every 2 s from its allocation. Each state is kept, as a code like any provider text, with its first and last read, and so is the first read that found the session's capabilities and a transport, which the SDK waits for before it connects. The reads stop at the first that finds the session `CLOSED`, or the second in a row that finds it gone, since one read answered 404 may be the coordinator's slip; or once the window closes, 120 s after the request: 15 s for allocation, `ACTIVE` and ready to come in (every paid run that timed it connected within 3.2 s of allocation), the 60 s cap, the 30 s Reactor documents after a session's last connection drops, and 15 s to spare. So the window ends past every end Reactor plausibly gives such a session, the cap and the 30 s after it counted from allocation, from `ACTIVE` or from ready, and the summary says by how much for each. An end is timed at the read that found the session `CLOSED`, or at the first of the reads in a row that found it gone. One more read then keeps what the coordinator says of the session: status, key names, state and codes. If that read finds it `CLOSED` where the watch did not, Reactor ended it after the watch's last read; a 404 there is an end only as the second in a row, as in the watch. If it finds it running, no end the watch found holds.
- **The end.** The key then ends the watched session whether or not Reactor did, and confirms the end as every check does; ending a session Reactor has closed ends nothing more. While an end is unconfirmed, the key tries twice more, 2 s apart, since nothing here can count on the cap; each unconfirmed try goes in the timeline, and the cleanup that ends what a failed or interrupted check left open tries the same way, for every session at once. Unlike every other check, its work runs past its session's cap: the cap is what it watches.
- **Q1 left open.** Nothing but Reactor, or the key after the watch, can end the watched session, so every end the watch finds is Reactor's answer. The record leaves whether Reactor ends such a session unanswered only where the reads cannot decide, and says so: when the read at the end finds running a session the watch found ended, or alone answers 404 after the watch's last read found it running.
- **Unknown creates.** A create that times out, meets a 5xx or names no session may still have allocated one, which the run never learns of. The summary then says which of the three creates it was and to look for its session in the dashboard, from the instant its token was first used, with any codes its reply held. It says so too when a crash stopped the run: a milestone is saved before each create, so the evidence shows which one went unanswered. A reply that names no session keeps its body, redacted, in the SDK's failure (`context.detail`), and the check keeps its codes as it keeps a refusal's. Each create and the record of its answer run as one step an interrupt waits for, up to the create's 15 s limit, so neither Ctrl-C nor a failure beside it can come between them.
- **Judged:** the watch completed, meaning a last read found the session, or found it gone; the spent token's second create was answered, with a session or a reply other than a 429, which a limit of the account's gives before it asks anything of the token; and every session the check made is confirmed ended. **Recorded:** when each create was sent and what it answered; who ended the watched session and when, also as an instant; when the window closes; each state with its first and last read; when the session first read connectable; the read at the end; and why the run leaves Q1 open, when it does. For each of the spent token's sessions: its reads after the second create, with its states as the watch keeps them, when it first read connectable, and when its hold began and was to end.
- **Billing.** The summary ends with a table for the dashboard, a row a session: who made it, when it was requested and allocated, its first `ACTIVE` read, its ready (the first read that found it connectable), the hold planned past it, who ended it and the two times its end lies between (for Reactor's, the last read that found it running and the first that found it ended; for the key's, its DELETE and the read that confirmed it; before a DELETE that found no session, its allocation and that DELETE), counted from the watched session's request, again from its own allocation and again from its ready, with blank cells for the dashboard's duration and charge. The spent token's sessions live about 10 s past ready, a few seconds more past allocation, and the watched one a minute or more, so together they tell billing per second from billing by the started minute or with a minimum, and billing from creation from billing from ready.
- **Rehearsed.** ReactorTest ends a session at its cap, counted from `ACTIVE`, whether or not anything connected, and refuses a spent single-session token `403 session_limit`; both are its own guesses. Rehearsal tests also run the check with the watched session ended 30 s after `ACTIVE` (`Expire`: Q1 answered), with the cap ignored (`IgnoreCap`: the key ends the session after the window), and with an end between the last read and the key's (`Expire`: the read at the end credits Reactor); with a spent token that allocates again (`IgnoreSessionLimit`: the key holds each of its sessions 10 s past its own ready) or names its session again (`RepeatSession`: only that one is held); with the spent token's session allocated too slowly to be ready within the 5 s wait (a 7 s allocation: it is held 10 s past the wait) or ended as it goes `ACTIVE` (`Expire` at 0 s: a read finds it `CLOSED`, the key ends it at once and the table credits Reactor); with the spent token's first create refused (`RefuseAllocation`: no second create, and the watch goes on), its second create hanging to its deadline (`StallAllocation`: the reads keep their schedule), refused 429 (`RefuseAllocation`: no answer), answered with a 5xx (`RefuseAllocation`) or with no session named (`UnnamedAllocation`: its codes are kept); with the watched session's create unknown or unnamed, which stops the check before the spent token is used; with the evidence a crash leaves while each of the three creates is unanswered; with a DELETE the session takes 3 s to honour (`SlowDelete`: the key tries again); with one read answered 404 for a running session (`MissingSession`: the watch goes on), two in a row that the read at the end contradicts, and a 404 on the read at the end alone (no end is credited, and Q1 is left open); and with the cap and the key's DELETE both ignored, which fails the check and tells the operator to end each session in the dashboard.

What one run cannot tell apart:

- The key's reads are activity a crashed creator would not make. If Reactor never ends the session, a run that reads it only at the end would tell whether the reads kept it alive.
- An end Reactor makes just as the window closes is told from the key's only by the read at the end.
- The spent token's sessions are read only from its second create's answer. One the first of those reads finds connectable reached ready sooner, at any time after its allocation, and its row cannot tell billing from creation from billing from ready; its create's reply keeps the state it was allocated in among its codes.

At the per-second rate: if Reactor ends the watched session at its cap, it bills about 60 s, $2.10; if nothing ends it, the key does about 120 s after the request, about $4.20. The spent token's session, and one its second create allocates, each bill about 10 s past ready, a few seconds more from creation: about $0.40 each. The holds are the worst cases: 155 s for the watched session, as the window (120 s), its last read (1 s), the read at the end (8 s) and the key's three tries (22 s) make 151 s; 59 s for the spent token's, as the key begins to end it 34 s after its request, 35 s if the wait's last read runs late, whatever its create (15 s), the second create (15 s), the wait (5 s) and the hold left, and its tries make 57 s; and 56 s for one the second create allocates, as that create (15 s), the wait and its last read (6 s) and the hold (10 s) make 31 s, and the key's tries 53 s. So $9.45 covers every session the run knows of at the per-second rate while one of the key's three tries ends each. A session made by a create whose outcome is unknown is not covered: the run never learns of it, and it bills until Reactor or someone at the dashboard ends it, which the summary says to do. Billed in whole minutes instead, three minutes, one and one come to $10.50, the check's ceiling, which the $10 total does not admit.

### Dropped

`dropped` asks hosted Reactor what the library cannot know: what becomes of a connected session whose connection is gone for good, as when the application that owns it crashes, with no DELETE and nobody reconnecting. Reactor's docs say a session waits 30 s for a connection, while paid `unconnected` found a session nothing ever connected to still `ACTIVE` 30 s after its allocation, and running to its 60 s cap ([its summary](./evidence/0.8.0-probe/summary.md)). An application's sessions may have no cap, and then only Reactor or the application's own DELETE stops their billing, so the answer bounds what a crash costs. The reviewed hold reserves 80 s, $2.80 at H3's per-second rate, so the check's `--budget-usd` is at least that and at most its ceiling, $4.20, in whole minutes.

- **The owner.** As `adoption`'s: under Node on the isolated native host in a paid run, and in this process on ReactorTest's peers in a rehearsal. It creates the session on a token for one session with no cap, reports it as soon as it allocates, connects it and sets it up, plays nothing, and reports that it is connected; the check then kills it with `SIGKILL`. The isolated peer's own process ends itself once its parent's channel closes, so the connection goes with the owner. The owner has 30 s to start and allocate, which bills nothing, and then 10 s to connect: paid runs connected within 3.2 s of allocation.
- **The token.** Hosted Reactor's echo of an uncapped token leaves its cap out, which says nothing, so the token's own claims decide: the check takes only a token for one session whose echo and claims state no cap, and `preflight` proves such a token for free.
- **The reads.** The API key reads the session every 2 s from the kill, keeping each state as a code with its first and last read, as `unconnected` does, until a read finds it `CLOSED`, or the second in a row finds it gone, or the window closes 60 s after the kill: twice the 30 s Reactor documents. A slow owner cannot lengthen the hold: the window closes 67 s after the allocation if that is sooner, so an owner that connects more than 7 s after its allocation gets a shorter window, which the summary gives.
- **The end.** A session a read finds `CLOSED` is read once more, for what the coordinator says of it. The key then ends the session whether or not Reactor did, and confirms the end, trying up to three times, 2 s apart. A session still running when the window closes was not ended by Reactor within the window: that is the answer, not a failure.
- **Judged:** the owner connected, the watch completed (its last read found the session, or found it gone), and the session is confirmed ended. **Recorded:** the owner's host; its allocation, connection and kill, the allocation and the kill also as instants; each state from the kill with its first and last read, so the first `INACTIVE` read; who ended the session, and between which two times: for Reactor's end, the last read that found it running and the first that found it ended; and the window.
- **Billing.** The summary ends with a row for the dashboard: the session's times from its allocation and from the kill, and blank cells for the dashboard's duration and charge.
- **The hold.** 80 s from the allocation: 1 s for the create's reply and the owner's report to reach the check, the window's latest end 67 s after the allocation, 1 s for its last read, and 11 s for the key's first two tries up to the second DELETE's answer (a DELETE and a read of 3 s each, 2 s apart). A third try, after two went unconfirmed, runs past the reservation, since nothing else ends an uncapped session; a run that cannot confirm the end says to end the session in the dashboard at once.
- **Rehearsed.** ReactorTest defaults to the hosted finding: once its last connection drops, a session reads `INACTIVE` until it is terminated, reconnected or capped. The default rehearsal ends it with the key once the window closes, 60 s after the kill and within the reviewed hold. Tests also opt into the documented 30 s end (`DisconnectEnds { after: Duration.seconds(30) }`), so the check credits Reactor; refuse the owner's connection (`RefuseConnect`), so the check fails and the key ends the session; and ignore the key's DELETE (`IgnoreDelete`), so the check fails and says to end the uncapped session in the dashboard now.

What one run cannot tell apart:

- The key's reads are activity a crashed application would not make. If Reactor keeps the session, a run that reads it only at the end would tell whether the reads kept it alive.
- The owner played nothing. Whether a clip playing or queued when the connection drops changes when Reactor ends the session is unmeasured.
- An end the reads found is known to within the 2 s between two reads.

At the per-second rate: ended 30 s after the kill, the session bills about 35 s, $1.23; not ended within the window, the key ends it about 65 s after its allocation, about $2.28.

## What we gather, and why

Everything comes from sessions the checks pay for anyway.

- **Environment:** commit, package versions, runtime and OS, and the operator's description of the network. It ties the evidence to the exact bytes.
- **Cost:** the published rate, each grant, time from allocation to confirmed end and an estimate that bills no less than Reactor does. The dashboard's charge beside the estimate settles how a started minute rounds.
- **CoordinatorClient facts:** cluster, zone, server version and transport, so Reactor can find its side of a run.
- **H3's contract:** the deployment's title and version, counts of each message type, unknown ones, duplicates, stale deliveries and diagnostics. Drift is fixed here and reported upstream.
- **Timing:** milestones, the library's own spans (only `reactor.*` attributes and `error.type`), acceptance, generation and start of each clip, and submission to Ready of each build. Spans also retain the six connection phase events (`reactor.connect.described`, `prepared`, `registered`, `offered`, `answered` and `ready`) at milliseconds since the run started, including phases of an attempt still open at a save. No other event names or event attributes are saved; older evidence has no `events` field. A one-second timer records its lateness on the monotonic clock, independently of session statistics, so a stalled runner can be distinguished from a slow reconnect.
- **Media:** frames, sizes, frame rate and intervals, lit and distinct frames, losses; audio blocks and levels; at each seam the longest pause, dark frames and the largest picture change against the ending clip's own motion.
- **Network:** one statistics sample a second and the local candidate type of the pair that carried the media. Every sample keeps the session's status and generation even when statistics are unavailable; vertical, turn, audio, tour and character also keep session events from allocation as tags, kinds, names, correlations and error reason tags, never message data or error text.
- **Adoption:** each read the adopting process makes of the session it takes over (`takeover`, `resume`, `tokens`, `adoption`), with the time since the owner died, and `adoption`'s reads while nothing is connected: status, state and key names. The first paid `tokens` run read `INACTIVE` 9 s after the kill, and the SDK then counted that session as ended; it was still running.
- **A session nothing connects to** (`unconnected`): each state the API key reads, as a code, and when, when the session could first be connected to, when the window closes, whether Reactor or the key ended it and when, and what the coordinator says of it at the end; what a second create on another token, spent by the session it made, answers, and the reads and hold of each session that token made; and each session's request, allocation, ready and end, for the dashboard's charges.
- **A session whose owner crashed** (`dropped`): the owner's allocation, connection and kill; every state read from the kill, the window, and whether Reactor or the key ended it, timed for the dashboard's billed duration.
- **An avatar call** (`avatar`): what each command met on the wire and how long it took, each refusal's `command_error` and the next `session_state`, every session event's tag and correlation, every `session_state`'s phase, booleans and numbers, each phase's frames and blocks, a call's sound level each 100 ms, and each transcript's speaker, `final` flag and length.
- **Termination:** the library's close report, then the coordinator's answer every half second until the session is `CLOSED` or gone.

The evidence never holds a credential, SDP, a candidate address, a frame, audio, provider text or the bytes or path of `avatar`'s photo. A save whose text would contain the API key or a session token writes nothing and stops the run. The two frames on either side of a playout seam are written at half size to a `reactor-seams-*` temporary directory for a person to look at; they are never part of the ledger. `showreel` writes its footage beside its evidence file, in a directory of its own that Git ignores; the evidence names the files.

## Making sure we get all of it

1. **One schema.** `Evidence.ts` defines the evidence and the sections each check must fill. A run that lacks one fails as incomplete, even when every criterion it evaluated passed.
2. **Saved as it happens.** The run claims a new file, recording its worst case, before any token exists, and saves after every milestone with an atomic replace. A crash still leaves the session ids, the reservation and everything observed so far. Ctrl-C interrupts the check, so its finalizers still close the sessions. A cleanup that ends what a failed check left open sends every DELETE at once, before it records any end, so a save that fails skips no session and no session waits on another's end.
3. **Bounded by the session.** All work finishes 10 s before the session's cap: within 40 s of allocation for a 50 s session. `dropped` observes an uncapped session within its reviewed 80 s hold, then confirms its end; a third cleanup try can exceed the reservation. `unconnected` reads its session until 120 s after the request, past the cap and 30 s counted from allocation, `ACTIVE` or ready, since the cap is what it watches, and then ends the session with the API key, trying up to three times; the sessions on its spent token it holds 10 s past their ready, within 15 s of that token's second create's answer, and then ends.
4. **Stops at uncertainty.** An unknown outcome, an allocation it cannot confirm, or an unconfirmed termination fails the run, never repeated by itself; the summary then says which session to confirm in the dashboard and by when its cap ends it. For `dropped`, whose session has no cap, it says to end it in the dashboard at once. For `unconnected`, whose question is whether the cap ends such a session, it says to end the session in the dashboard instead, and after a create whose outcome is unknown, to look there for a session it may have allocated, with any codes the create's reply held. `avatar` records a command's unknown outcome and goes on: its commands bill nothing beyond the session's time, which it ends either way, and one of them may never be answered.
5. **Free preflight.** `preflight` checks the live rate of one check's model against that check's budget and the ledger, and a token minted for the model and its granted limits (minting allocates nothing): `vertical`'s, unless `--check` names another check. It also checks that the native library loads, and that `adoption`'s owner can run: `node` 22.18 or newer, and the isolated owner command, which given no grant must build its native peer and exit on the missing grant (its error output is never read). It then prints the token probes: what a 15 s and a 7 h token live, what an uncapped and a three-session token echo, the `/tokens` reply's key names and types, and what a bind of an unknown session and the API key reading or ending one answer. Each is one request that allocates nothing; only statuses, codes, numbers and key names are kept. Every probe request, and every read with the key, refuses a redirect and sends no cookies, as the client's coordinator does, so a credential goes only where it was sent.
6. **Rehearsed end to end.** `rehearse <check>` runs the same program against `ReactorTest` at the timing paid runs measured, under a test clock, so it takes a moment and repeats exactly. The takeover's owner runs in this process there, and so does `adoption`'s, which a paid run starts under Node on the isolated native host: a kill cuts the owner's network before interrupting it, so it cannot close or terminate what it held. CI rehearses every check and these failure paths as `ReactorTest` faults: a lost enqueue reply, a termination confirmed only after the library's read, black and frozen video, missing audio and an over-granting token; `cut`'s moderation three ways (a verdict, a session ended with no verdict, a prompt moderation lets through); a refused clip in `adoption`, whose later steps still run, and an `adoption` owner that fails before streaming, whose session is still ended; `show`'s loss both ways, by a verdict and by the API key; and `unconnected` with its session ended 30 s in, with its cap ignored, and with an end just after its last watch read; with a spent token that allocates again or names its session again, whose first create is refused, or whose second create hangs to its deadline, is refused 429, meets a 5xx or names no session; with the spent token's session never ready within its wait, and found ended after the second create; with the watched session's create unknown or naming no session; with the evidence a crash leaves while each create is unanswered; with a slow DELETE; with a read answered 404 for a running session, once, twice with the read at the end contradicting, and on the read at the end alone; and with both its cap and the key's DELETE ignored; `showreel` with its first scene's build failed; `showreel`'s recording through a real ffmpeg, when one is installed; and `avatar` with no picture, each call doing all it does for one. It also starts `adoption`'s paid owner command under Node from the TypeScript sources, as far as its help: the portable gate has no staged addon for the isolated peer. A rehearsal checks every save but writes only its claim and its last: its clock moves while it runs, so a file written mid-check would move the simulated session's time.

A rehearsal cannot prove what only hosted Reactor decides: how it notices a vanished owner and accepts a new connection, what it does with a session nothing connects to, its build and seam timing, billing, relay behaviour, or what a viewer sees.

### rejoin

`rejoin` asks what one reconnect during a live Vidu S2-Avatar call does. Its session is capped at
120 s, reserving at most $0.84 at the reviewed 70 credits a second or by the started minute. Work
ends within 110 s of allocation; at the slower paid avatar run's timing, the call should end
about 70 s in. A paid run requires the operator's photo through `--avatar-image`, checked before
the evidence claim. Only its type and size are kept.

The provider makes the avatar and starts a call with a greeting. The check waits up to 10 s for
video frames and audio blocks, then reconnects once, even when the picture is absent. The reconnect has a
45 s harness bound, beyond the SDK's 30 s negotiation deadline. Its start, end, outcome and
generation are saved before further work. Fresh decoded readers observe the resulting generation
for up to 10 s; the record keeps first frame and block delays, counts, the provider's phase and
diagnostic reason tags, allocation-time session events, connection phases and network samples.
The summary also reports the largest timer lateness whose measurement interval overlaps the
reconnect. It then attempts `endCall` within 20 s and closes the session, with key cleanup as a
backup. It passes on a live picture, a successful reconnect within 30 s and a confirmed session
end. Media after reconnect is recorded evidence, not a pass criterion. A rehearsal exercises this
workflow on ReactorTest; it cannot establish hosted reconnect timing or media behaviour.

## Runbook

Qualify the published bytes from a machine whose network carries WebRTC media (outbound UDP, or TURN over TCP or TLS), with Bun 1.4.2, in a scratch project with the release candidate:

```sh
mkdir reactor-qualification && cd reactor-qualification && npm init -y > /dev/null
npm pkg set type=module
npm install reactor-effect-client@<version> reactor-effect-native@<version> \
  effect@4.0.0 @effect/platform-node@4.0.0
cp -R <this repository>/integration/hosted ./hosted

# Free.
bun hosted/main.ts rehearse vertical
export REACTOR_API_KEY=...
bun hosted/main.ts preflight --total-budget-usd 5.25 --ledger ledger

# Paid, one at a time. Read each result before the next.
bun hosted/main.ts run vertical --budget-usd 1.75 --total-budget-usd 5.25 \
  --ledger evidence/<version> --network "home fiber, no VPN" --i-authorize-paid-sessions
bun hosted/main.ts run renewal --budget-usd 3.50 --total-budget-usd 5.25 \
  --ledger evidence/<version> --network "home fiber, no VPN" --i-authorize-paid-sessions

bun hosted/main.ts summarize evidence/<version> > evidence/<version>/summary.md
```

A run exits 0 when it passes, 1 when it fails, and 2 when it refused before claiming its evidence file, and so before spending anything. To re-run a fix before it is released, run `bun --no-env-file integration/hosted/main.ts` from a built checkout instead; the evidence records the commit, so a run refuses when `reactor-effect-client` or `reactor-effect-native` resolves to a checkout whose build is older than its sources.

After a failure, read its reasons and fix the cause before running it again. To run a fix before it is released, run from a checkout of it: `bun run build`, then, if the native sources are unchanged, copy the published candidate's addon from its platform package into `packages/native/npm/<platform>/` and run `node packages/native/scripts/stage.mjs` on it, which refuses it unless its `sourceSha256` matches the checkout. The evidence records the commit. After an unknown outcome or an unconfirmed termination, confirm in the Reactor dashboard that the session ended. After all the runs, compare the dashboard's charges with each run's estimate and note any difference in `summary.md`.

### Moderation

Reactor screens prompts and reference images and terminates a session given flagged content (resources › Content moderation); a session it ends "can" carry a reason (reactor-runtime 3.2.0 notes), so none is promised. `cut --moderation-prompt-file <file>` ends the cut with an item carrying that file's prompt, queued behind a 15 s guard clip so it could air only after the watch; if it comes back Ready unflagged it is withdrawn. The playout may open no second session and fails after one moderation. `show --moderation-prompt-file <file>` ends its second session the same way, the flagged item queued behind a 10 s guard clip on air and a Ready clip; its playout may lose two sessions to moderation before it fails. The evidence records whether the enqueue replied, any verdict (its action, categories, input kind and command, and whether its request id is the enqueue's), the session's statuses and control messages, the playout's events, and the coordinator's read afterwards. The prompt is a run secret: it is never logged or saved, and a save that would hold it fails. Keep the file outside the repository. Submitting content meant to be flagged is the account owner's decision under Reactor's Acceptable Use Policy.

## Sharing the results

Commit the paid evidence files and `summary.md` under `evidence/<version>/` in the pull request that records the qualification. Session ids stay in: they let Reactor find its side of each run, and without a token they are useless. The release notes quote the summary; contract drift and surprising termination or cap behaviour go to Reactor with the session ids and times involved.

The ledgers up to 0.7.0 are records in the v1 schema, written by the harness those releases shipped, under the checks' old names. The CLI reads only the v2 schema, so each release from 0.8.0 starts a ledger of its own.
