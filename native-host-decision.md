# Native host decision: keep, shrink or replace the Rust bridge

This document is about `reactor-effect-native` (`packages/native`) at `reactor-effect-client@2f12cc5`. At the time of writing, `packages/native` is unchanged at this branch's base, `749d184`.

It covers four things: whether the Rust bridge is the right way for a Node or Bun process to receive Reactor media, the alternatives, the measurements behind the choice, and what to change.

Everything here was executed on one Linux x64 host with no credentials. The host had 4 vCPUs, 15 GB of RAM, Ubuntu 24.04 and kernel 6.18. The toolchain was:

- Node 24.15.0, from the Node.js distribution and SHA-256-checked;
- Bun 1.4.2, digest-checked;
- Rust 1.90.0;
- Clang 21.1.8, from its GitHub release, because apt.llvm.org was refused by the egress policy;
- ffmpeg 6.1.1.

No hosted Reactor endpoint, paid session or upstream pull request was used. The spikes live in [`spikes/native-host/`](spikes/native-host/), and their raw output is in `spikes/native-host/results/`.

## 1. Recommendation

**Shrink the bridge (option B).**

- **Protocol owner:** this library's TypeScript `Session`, still the only one.
- **WebRTC stack:** reactor-webrtc on Reactor's libwebrtc prebuilt, bound through Koffi.
- **ABI 3:** replace the async probe/copy polls with bounded native queues, a Koffi readiness callback and one synchronous, single-copy take per frame. Use one factory per process and typed failure classes.
- **Upstream strategy:** propose reactor-webrtc patches for typed `RTCErrorType`, remote MID and ICE state (drafted), audio capture time and decode errors. Carry them in a `rev`-pinned fork only if they stall.
- **Main reason (measured):** under Bun 1.4.2, the-show's runtime, today's bridge delivered 16% of frames. With two sessions on Node it lost 44% of audio to libuv-pool contention. The spike delivered every frame on both runtimes, at about a third of the CPU. No alternative works unmodified. werift keeps libwebrtc at 300 kbps until its TWCC is fixed. node-datachannel has no loss recovery. @roamhq/wrtc aborts under Bun. The official engine takes the protocol out of TypeScript and exposes no drop counters.
- **Decision needed from the product owner:** is a Koffi-loaded bridge over Reactor's reactor-webrtc and Reactor-published libwebrtc "independent" enough (§2)?

## 3. Factory verdict

**Verdict.** One factory per peer showed no crash in any reproduction at the bridge's pin (`bebf63e`) on Linux x64, including the-show's renewal pattern.

Each run below is one process; a crash would show as a signal exit status. [`run-factory-hazard.sh`](spikes/native-host/run-factory-hazard.sh) drives the modes in [`factory_hazard.rs`](spikes/native-host/rust/src/bin/factory_hazard.rs):

| Mode        | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                     | Processes × iterations      | Crashes, panics or hangs |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| `shared`    | One factory for both ends (the documented contract; control)                                                                                                                                     | 20 × 50 sessions with media | 0                        |
| `per-peer`  | The bridge pattern. The offerer and answerer each create a factory, both are alive at once, and media flows.                                                                                     | 20 × 50                     | 0                        |
| `renewal`   | The-show's renewal. Session k+1 is opened on a new factory while k streams. Session k and its factory are destroyed while k+1 streams, and the replacement is asserted to keep receiving frames. | 20 × 30                     | 0                        |
| `drop-live` | The client factory is dropped while its connection still receives media                                                                                                                          | 20 × 30                     | 0                        |
| `seq`       | Create factory, connection and offer, then destroy; repeat                                                                                                                                       | 20 × 2,000                  | 0                        |
| `churn`     | 4 threads, each creating and destroying factories concurrently                                                                                                                                   | 20 × 500 × 4                | 0                        |

In total that is 120 processes, about 83,000 factory lifecycles and about 3,200 media sessions (1,184 s). Raw data: `spikes/native-host/results/factory-summary.tsv`.

**Through the shipped artifact.** [`renewal-stress.mjs`](spikes/native-host/js/renewal-stress.mjs) runs the renewal through `NativePeer`, Koffi and the staged `.so`, so every replacement creates a new factory inside the bridge. It completed 30 renewal cycles (31 factories) on both runtimes. Old-session shutdown took p50 21 ms and max 38 ms on Node 24, and p50 131 ms and max 170 ms on Bun 1.4.2. The replacement kept receiving frames through every drain.

**Why the upstream docs still say otherwise.** `docs/architecture.md:75-87` says that a second factory created before the first is "fully destroyed reliably races those threads and segfaults". The Python binding enforces a single live factory with a global `AtomicBool` (`reactor-webrtc-py/src/lib.rs:33-50`). Its test fixture says repeated create and destroy segfaults "on the Nth cycle" (`tests/conftest.py:7-22`). The Rust tests, however, create several factories in one process, one after another (`tests/frame_metadata.rs:264, 326`). So do the bridge's own loopback test (`lib.rs:2056`) and the official engine, which creates one factory per `reactor_create` handle (`reactor-ffi/src/peer.rs:337-359`; `lib.rs:884-887`). The same revision has a guarantee that the factory stays alive while any object it created is alive (`docs/architecture.md:40-73`, "Before this guarantee existed…"). It also carries fixed lifetime races with regression tests (`tests/sdp_bridge_race.rs`, `tests/factory_lifetime.rs`). The warning probably predates that work. That is inference, not verified.

**Action.**

- **Bridge:** there is no crash to fix. The shrunk bridge still uses one leaked process-wide factory (`spikes/native-host/bridge-b/src/lib.rs`, `shared_factory`). This follows the upstream rule and Reactor's own runtime ("keeps a single process-wide factory behind a lock", `docs/architecture.md:85-87`). It also saves a factory's signaling, worker, network and audio-playout threads (`reactor_webrtc.cpp:630-639, 329-342`) for every additional peer. **Measured** peak thread count with two sessions: 29 for A, 26 for B, which adds one notifier thread per peer.
- **Upstream:** the useful patch is evidence, not enforcement. Offer the `factory_hazard` modes as a regression test, and ask for the crashing reproduction behind the docs and the Python guard.
- **Not verified:** macOS arm64 (no host), and older reactor-webrtc versions such as the 0.16.0 inside the C++ 2.0.2 engine.

## 5. Spike results

**Setup.** Every spike receives from the same far peer, [`far_peer`](spikes/native-host/rust/src/bin/far_peer.rs). It is libwebrtc through reactor-webrtc `bebf63e`, the stack Reactor's server runs, and it uses one factory. It sends:

- 1344×768 BGRA at 24 fps. The pattern is a moving gradient with a noisy band, so VP8 needs real bits, and the sender's range is 0.3–8 Mbit/s.
- 48 kHz mono PCM in 10 ms blocks.
- A 16-byte `user_data` in each frame's metadata trailer: wall-clock microseconds, then a sequence number. Latency is receive time minus that stamp, on one host clock.

It answers with its gathered candidates, as Reactor does; the bridge's C ABI has no remote-candidate call. It echoes both data channels. VP8 was negotiated in every run (werift's offer lists only VP8).

**Schedule** (`js/harness.mjs`):

1. Connect.
2. Receive for 60 s, sending a `control` round trip every second.
3. At 20 s, block the JS thread for 250 ms (2 s in the stall runs).
4. At 40 s, interrupt the video reader. For A this is an Effect fiber interruption while a Koffi poll is in flight. For B and F, where no foreign call is in flight on the media path, it detaches the reader.
5. Shut down.

**Measurements:**

- **CPU and RSS:** the client process's CPU from `process.cpuUsage()`, plus ffmpeg children from `/proc`; the far peer is excluded. RSS is sampled at 4 Hz.
- **Event-loop lag:** a 20 ms timer.
- **Threadpool latency:** a libuv-threadpool probe (`fs.stat` every 50 ms), because Koffi's async calls share that pool on Node.

With two peers, today's bridge keeps 6 polls in flight (p50 6, max 8), each blocking for up to 100 ms, on Node's 4-thread libuv pool. Each packet also needs two more pool calls (probe and copy). The pool saturates:

- the audio reader falls behind and the native drop-oldest queue discards 44%;
- control-channel sends queue behind polls (RTT p50 30 ms against 1.3 ms);
- every other pool user in the process waits, including `fs`, `dns.lookup` and `crypto`: the probe's p95 rises from 0.5 to 26 ms.

The drops are counted, which is the bridge's design goal. But at the required load they are not rare events.

A micro-benchmark isolates the mechanism (`js/koffi-async-bench.mjs`). An unrelated Koffi async call takes 55 µs with one peer's three blocking polls in flight. With two peers' six, it waits **90 ms** (106 ms on Bun) for a pool thread.

werift did not recover:

- **Keyframes:** it never asks for one on its own. Its automatic PLI is commented out (`receiver/nack.ts:85`). Its depacketizer drops a frame at the first sequence gap (`depacketizer.ts`, `frameBroken`) instead of waiting for the retransmission.
- **NACK storm:** its NACK loop re-requests every lost packet every 5 ms, up to 10 times, whatever the RTT (`receiver/nack.ts:14-52`). At 80 ms RTT, each loss drew about 10 retransmissions: 10,811 RTX packets for 1,139 drops. The storm congested the path, pushing data-channel RTT to p50 1.5 s.

Making F viable on real networks means writing a receiver on top of werift: RTT-aware NACK, keyframe requests, and frame assembly that tolerates loss. libwebrtc already does all of this.

**Why werift stalls libwebrtc.** werift 0.24.4's receiver TWCC (`packages/webrtc/src/media/receiver/receiverTwcc.ts`) has three problems:

- it states a reference time quantized to 64 ms, but measures each feedback's first delta from the unquantized previous arrival, so every feedback shifts the arrival timeline by up to 64 ms;
- it reports lost packets inside the run-length chunks, instead of as "not received";
- it does not unwrap the 16-bit transport sequence.

libwebrtc's delay-based estimator reads the shifted timeline as congestion and stays at its floor. A conforming encoder (`--fix-twcc`, about 60 lines) lifts it to 1.7 Mbit/s but not to 3–10. werift timestamps packets on the JS thread after SRTP decryption (`microTime()` in `handleTWCC`), not at the socket, so JS scheduling jitter still looks like queueing delay. Reaching the required load would need both fixes upstream in werift, or Reactor's server pinning a minimum bitrate (unverified; §8). With a forced 6 Mbit/s floor, werift decrypts and depacketizes the stream in 64% of a core. Adding ffmpeg's 23% puts it at 2.2 times B's CPU.

Recovery rule, independent of this choice: today a transient `disconnected` state fails the connection at once (`session.ts:236`), and so does the official engine (`reactor.rs:734-753`). libwebrtc's `disconnected` can recover. With 78–253 ms RTT over relay candidates, `disconnected` should start a bounded grace period (ICE consent freshness, about 30 s by default) before failing with `IceFailed` or `TransportFailed`.

Draft Rust enum for the bridge's C ABI (ABI 3), replacing the `code` strings of `lib.rs:44-84`:

```rust
/// Failure class carried by every nonzero status of the ABI-3 bridge. Values
/// are stable; the TypeScript host maps them one-to-one to ErrorCode.
#[repr(i32)]
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum FailureClass {
    InvalidInput = -1,
    Unknown = -2,
    Overflow = -3,
    SdpRejected = -4,
    IceFailed = -5,
    TransportFailed = -6,
    ChannelClosed = -7,
    DecodeFailed = -8,
    Shutdown = -9,
    Timeout = -10,
}

/// Written to the caller's out-parameter beside the status; no JSON.
#[repr(C)]
pub struct NativeFailure {
    pub class: FailureClass,
    /// webrtc::RTCErrorType when the class came from libwebrtc, else 0.
    pub rtc_kind: i32,
    /// Stable operation id (prepare = 1, answer = 2, …).
    pub operation: u32,
    pub message_len: u32,
    /// UTF-8, truncated; for diagnostics only and never matched on.
    pub message: [u8; 240],
}

impl From<reactor_webrtc::Error> for FailureClass {
    fn from(error: reactor_webrtc::Error) -> Self {
        use reactor_webrtc::{Error, RtcErrorKind as K};
        match error {
            Error::Rtc { operation, kind, .. }
                if operation.ends_with("_description") || operation.starts_with("create_") =>
            {
                match kind {
                    K::NoPeerConnection => Self::Shutdown,
                    _ => Self::SdpRejected,
                }
            }
            Error::Rtc { operation: "add_ice_candidate", .. } => Self::InvalidInput,
            Error::Rtc { kind: K::NetworkError, .. } => Self::TransportFailed,
            Error::Timeout(_) => Self::Timeout,
            Error::NotFound(_) => Self::InvalidInput,
            _ => Self::Unknown,
        }
    }
}
```

TypeScript side: the host maps `FailureClass` into these additions to the `ErrorCode` literal set:

```ts
// packages/client/src/errors.ts: five additions.
"SdpRejected", "IceFailed", "TransportFailed", "ChannelClosed", "DecodeFailed",
```

The bridge also keeps the ICE-versus-DTLS inference in TypeScript, so it works before patch 2. On `state: "failed"`, it reads `stats()` once. If any `candidate-pair` has `state: "succeeded"` or `nominated: true`, the failure is `TransportFailed`; otherwise it is `IceFailed`. The stats path already exposes pair state (`lib.rs:1086-1111`).

**Smallest paid check the recommendation needs.** Run it once B has landed. Do not run it before.

- **Inputs:**
  - `reactor-effect-native` ABI 3 built from this branch's successor, on Linux x64 glibc under Bun 1.4.2;
  - one model the-show already uses;
  - two sessions opened by the renewal pattern: the second opens 60 s after the first, and the first drains for 30 s.
- **Cost bound:** at most 2 sessions, 5 minutes of wall time in total, so at most 10 session-minutes at the account's rate, in one region (ap-southeast, the worst measured RTT). Abort at the first failed criterion.
- **Pass when all of these hold:**
  1. The answer SDP is recorded, with its codec and the `a=x-reactor-frame-metadata:1` line.
  2. Each session, measured over its stable 60 s window:
     - averages at least 23 decoded frames per second at 1344×768;
     - has at least 99% of frames with a nonzero `frameId`;
     - keeps p95 age below 400 ms, measured from the sender's `timestampMicros` across the RTT;
     - keeps native `dropped*` at no more than 1% at 250 ms stalls.
  3. The `inbound-rtp` NACK count is nonzero or the loss is zero, and no freeze lasts longer than 1 s.
  4. Both data channels complete a command round trip.
  5. The renewal overlap delivers to both sessions, and the drained session shuts down in under 2 s.
  6. The TURN-forced second session connects through a `relay` candidate.
- **Fail when:** any criterion misses, or a native crash or unclassified `Native` error occurs.
